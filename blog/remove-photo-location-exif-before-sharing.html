How to Remove Location and Camera Data (EXIF) from Photos Before Sharing

4 min read · Updated October 2026

A photo you take at home can quietly carry the exact coordinates of your home. Before you post or send it, it is worth removing that hidden data.

What is EXIF data?

EXIF is information stored inside an image file: date and time, camera or phone model, settings, and — if location is switched on — GPS coordinates. Most people never see it, but anyone who receives the original file can read it.

When it matters

  • Selling items online from home.
  • Sharing photos of children, a house or a workplace.
  • Sending photos to strangers or posting the original file on a forum.

Some apps remove this data when you upload; others pass the original file along. Do not assume — check or strip it yourself.

Remove it in your browser

  1. Open Strip Photo Privacy Data.
  2. Add your photos.
  3. Download the cleaned copies. The picture looks the same; the hidden data is gone.

Also reduce future leaks

  • In your camera settings, turn off location tagging if you do not need it.
  • Keep the cleaned copy for sharing and the original for your own records.

Frequently asked questions

Will the photo quality change?

The picture is kept as it is; only the metadata is removed.

Do screenshots have EXIF data?

They usually carry less, but they can still hold dates or device information.

Is the photo uploaded?

No. The cleaning is done in your browser.
